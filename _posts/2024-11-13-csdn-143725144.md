excerpt: "A residue class that has a multiplicative inverse, multiplied by an arbitrary integer, is still a residue class that has a multiplicative inverse. 3 and 6 are not coprime; there is no inverse of 3, so dividing by 3 cannot be converted into multiplying by the inverse of 3. Here 3/5 mod 2 is equivalent to 3 multiplied by the multiplicative inverse of 5 mod 2. At this point a no longer needs to be coprime with the modulus; it only needs to be in the residue class. Looking at it another way, dividing both sides by 3 does not divide the modulus. If the number to divide by is coprime with the modulus, you can divide directly. Since the other nj are factors of ni*, the result modulo is 0. You can take the modulus at any time, but in the end you must reduce it below the modulus. The multiplicative inverse of 1 is always itself, no matter what the modulus is. With different moduli, the multiplicative inverse of the same number is different. When dividing both sides, you can only divide by a number coprime with the modulus."
