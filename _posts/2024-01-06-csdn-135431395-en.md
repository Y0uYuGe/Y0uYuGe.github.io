## The Oldest Counting Method
One vertical stroke corresponds to one apple, but when the quantity is large, too many strokes must be drawn

Later, a horizontal line was used to represent 5, so fewer strokes had to be drawn

The larger the number, the more cumbersome its symbolic representation

## Generalization: Base-r Counting
"Symbols" refers to 0, 1, 2, 3…… these
Note
In binary, 1.1+0.1=10.0
In octal, 5.4+0.4=6.0
In hexadecimal, 5.8+0.8=6.0
For a base-r number, remember that the fractional part also carries 1 whenever it reaches r
Note that the base is a count

## Binary <---> Octal, Hexadecimal
****Binary to octal and hexadecimal****
If the fractional part does not have enough digits, pad zeros at the end; if the integer part does not have enough digits, pad zeros at the front
Pad until you have enough digits for the group; conversion still works by taking the right side of the grouped digits as the low order and the left side as the high order, then converting to the value in the corresponding base
