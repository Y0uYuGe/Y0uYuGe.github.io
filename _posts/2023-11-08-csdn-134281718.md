# Principles of memory chips
 Memory cell: a physical device that stores one binary bit (the basic unit circuit of memory)
 MOS transistor: think of it as an electrically controlled switch; when the input voltage reaches a certain threshold, the MOS transistor turns on (a semiconductor component)
 In the memory cell circuit, a voltage difference across the capacitor represents 1, otherwise 0; the MOS transistor controls whether to read or write
 When the MOS transistor is at high voltage, the circuit is connected, and the data in the capacitor is read out to the rightmost end, or the signal at the rightmost end is written into the capacitor
 When the MOS transistor is at low voltage, the circuit is disconnected, preserving the signal in the capacitor
