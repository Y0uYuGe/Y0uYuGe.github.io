# Where Do Errors Come From
- Noise usually refers to any unexpected, random signal interference, which may come from a variety of physical sources, such as thermal noise in electronic devices, environmental interference with wireless signals, and so on.

## An Example of Improving the Signal-to-noise Ratio at the Sensor Level to Reduce the Random Noise of the Line Itself

In a communication system, the signal attenuates as it is transmitted through the channel, and the interference and noise on the channel also affect the signal, causing signal distortion. To ensure that the signal can be correctly decoded when it reaches the receiving end without too much error, measures need to be taken to improve the signal-to-noise ratio. Details are as follows:

Improving transmission means: for example, using optical cables or coaxial cables instead of traditional copper cables, because these media provide higher transmission quality and can effectively reduce signal loss during transmission and external electromagnetic interference.

Increasing device capability: for example, using a low-noise amplifier (LNA) at the sensor output to enhance signal strength, or using a digital filter in the signal processing stage to remove noise, thereby improving the quality of the signal.
## Thermal Noise and Impulse Noise
Impulse noise and thermal noise are two different kinds of interference, each with its own characteristics and solutions. Details are as follows:

- Impulse noise:

Characteristics: impulse noise is usually a sudden interference caused by external factors, possibly due to electromagnetic interference or power switch transients. Its amplitude is relatively large, and it may cause multiple adjacent bits in data transmission to be in error at the same time.

Solution: to solve the problem of impulse noise, error-correcting coding techniques can be used, such as parity check codes, cyclic redundancy check (CRC), or more complex forward error correction (FEC) codes. These codes can detect and correct at the receiving end the burst errors caused by impulse noise.
- Thermal noise:

Characteristics: thermal noise, also known as white noise, is caused by the thermal vibration of electrons in conductors and exists in all electronic devices and transmission media. It is the result of temperature changes, but is not affected by frequency changes. Thermal noise cannot be eliminated; it is an unavoidable part of a communication system.

Solution: although thermal noise cannot be completely eliminated, its impact can be reduced by increasing the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). This can be achieved by using low-noise amplifiers (LNAs), high-quality cables and connectors, and filters in signal processing. In addition, increasing the transmission power of the signal can also offset the influence of thermal noise to a certain extent.

## Error-detecting Code - Parity Check Code
The check bit is added; whether it is 0 or 1 depends on whether odd parity or even parity is used.
Odd parity: correct if the number of 1s is odd.
Even parity: correct if the number of 1s is even.

If an odd number of 1s is found, it is still possible that an even number of 1s were lost. So the accuracy is only half.

## Notes
The statement that all frames received by the data link layer at the receiving end are error-free is generally correct: "received" here has the meaning of "finally accepted", because frames with errors are not finally accepted. But note that the bits corresponding to the remainder may also be different, so it is still possible for errors to exist even when everything appears error-free; however, because the probability is small, it is considered error-free.

CRC checking achieves error-free bits (not one hundred percent), but there is no guarantee against frame loss or duplication. So it is not the case that whatever is sent will be received; it is not reliable transmission.
