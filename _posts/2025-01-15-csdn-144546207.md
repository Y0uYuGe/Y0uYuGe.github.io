# Basic Concepts
### Document
- **Definition**: input documents in the system. These documents represent either individual rows in a CSV file or individual .txt files.
- **Examples**:
  - A row of data in a CSV file: `Name,Age,Location;John,25,New York`
  - Content of an individual .txt file: `This is a sample text document.`

### TextUnit
- **Definition**: the chunks of text to be analyzed. The size of these chunks, their overlap, and whether they respect any data boundaries can be configured below. A common use case is setting `CHUNK_BY_COLUMNS` to `id` so that there is a one-to-many relationship between documents and TextUnits, rather than a many-to-many relationship.
- **Examples**:
  - Suppose there is a long document: "This is a sample text. This text is another example text. This is just another text."
    - If `CHUNK_BY_COLUMNS` is set to `id`, it can be split into multiple text chunks:
      - Text chunk 1: "This is a sample text."
      - Text chunk 2: "This text is another example text."
      - Text chunk 3: "This is just another text."

### Entity
- **Definition**: entities extracted from TextUnits. These entities represent people, places, events, or other entity models you provide.
- **Examples**:
  - Text chunk: "John lives in New York and works at Google."
    - Entities:
      - Person: John
      - Place: New York
      - Organization: Google

### Relationship
- **Definition**: the relationship between two entities. These relationships are generated with covariates.
- **Examples**:
  - Text chunk: "John lives in New York and works at Google."
    - Relationships:
      - John (person) -> lives in (relationship) -> New York (place)
      - John (person) -> works at (relationship) -> Google (organization)

### Covariate
- **Definition**: extracted claim information containing statements about entities that may be time-bounded.
- **Examples**:
  - Text chunk: "John lived in New York from 2010 to 2015."
    - Covariates:
      - Time range: 2010 to 2015
      - Statement: John lived in New York

### Claim
- **Definition**: represents a positive factual statement with an evaluation status and time limits; referred to as covariates elsewhere.
- **Examples**:
  - Text chunk: "John lived in New York from 2010 to 2015."
    - Claims:
      - John lived in New York (evaluation status: true)
      - Time range: 2010 to 2015

### Community Report (a community is a subgraph)
- **Definition**: once entities are generated, we perform hierarchical community detection on them and generate a report for each community in that hierarchy.
- **Examples**:
  - Suppose we have the following entities and relationships:
    - Entities: John, New York, Google
    - Relationships: John lives in New York, John works at Google
  - Community reports:
    - Community 1: John, New York
    - Community 2: John, Google
    - Reports:
      - Community 1 report: John lives in New York
      - Community 2 report: John works at Google

### Node
- **Definition**: contains layout information for rendering the graph view of the embedded and indexed entities and documents.
- **Examples**:
  - In a graph view, nodes can represent different entities:
    - Node 1: John
    - Node 2: New York
    - Node 3: Google
  - These nodes are connected by edges, representing the relationships between entities.

# Principles of Index Construction
##  Document -> TextUnit

There are certain requirements for the text chunk size: if it is too large, the contextual information is not well preserved; if it is too small, there are too many chunks to process and it takes a long time.
![Image](/images/csdn/2025/csdn-144546207/image-001-668a92c855.png)
## Text Units -> Graph Extraction

Entities and relationships are extracted from text units and summaries are generated; claims are more detailed summaries.
![Image](/images/csdn/2025/csdn-144546207/image-002-ebd1ca9a4d.png)
## Building Communities
Communities are subgraphs with similarities. Graph embedding works by generating vector representations of nodes; these vectors can reflect the positions of nodes in the graph and their relationships with other nodes.
![Image](/images/csdn/2025/csdn-144546207/image-003-e861e305c9.png)
## Community Summarization
Generate a report for each community; each community report is summarized by an LLM for quick reference. Finally, a vector representation of the community is generated.
![Image](/images/csdn/2025/csdn-144546207/image-004-7318c4068a.png)

## Document Processing
Documents are linked to text units. Document embedding is done by splitting a document into multiple parts, embedding each part, and then computing the average of these embeddings to obtain a vector representation of the entire document. This method can capture implicit relationships between documents.
![Image](/images/csdn/2025/csdn-144546207/image-005-95fe9fbabd.png)
# Principles of the Query Pipeline

## Local Search

 ![Image](/images/csdn/2025/csdn-144546207/image-006-0c0810aa65.png)

### Data Flow Overview

1. **User Query**
   - The user enters a query.

2. **Extracted Entities**
   - Entities are extracted from the user query using Description Embedding technology.

3. **Conversation History**
   - Records and uses conversation history information.

### Main Processing Pipeline

#### 1. Entity-Text Unit Mapping
   - **Candidate Text Units**
     - Candidate text units are generated from the entity-to-text-unit mapping.
   - **Prioritized Text Units**
     - Candidate text units are prioritized through ranking and filtering.

#### 2. Entity-Report Mapping
   - **Candidate Community Reports**
     - Candidate community reports are generated from the entity-to-report mapping.
   - **Prioritized Community Reports**
     - Candidate community reports are prioritized through ranking and filtering.

#### 3. Entity-Entity Relationships
   - **Candidate Entities**
     - Candidate entities are generated from the entity-to-entity relationship mapping.
   - **Prioritized Entities**
     - Candidate entities are prioritized through ranking and filtering.

#### 4. Entity-Relationship Mapping
   - **Candidate Relationships**
     - Candidate relationships are generated from the entity-to-relationship mapping.
   - **Prioritized Relationships**
     - Candidate relationships are prioritized through ranking and filtering.

#### 5. Entity-Covariate Mapping
   - **Candidate Covariates**
     - Candidate covariates are generated from the entity-to-covariate mapping.
   - **Prioritized Covariates**
     - Candidate covariates are prioritized through ranking and filtering.

### Final Response

- All prioritized text units, community reports, entities, relationships, and covariates are finally merged to generate the final response.

### Conversation History

- The conversation history is recorded throughout the process and used for subsequent query processing.

### Summary

This process extracts entities from the user query through multiple mapping and prioritization steps, and generates candidate text units, community reports, entities, relationships, and covariates based on these entities. Finally, after prioritization, these candidates are merged with the chat history to generate the final response. The conversation history is utilized throughout the process to provide more accurate and personalized search results.

## Global Search
It traverses each community to look up relevant information, scores and filters what is found, then concatenates the content to fill the prompt, and finally calls the LLM.
