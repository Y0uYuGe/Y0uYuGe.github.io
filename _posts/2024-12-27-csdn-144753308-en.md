# Summary

- From the mid-1970s to the mid-1990s, DES was the dominant symmetric encryption algorithm.
Because the 56-bit key used by DES is no longer secure, the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) was later developed to replace it. AES uses longer keys (128, 192, 256 bits) and provides higher security.
- Standard DES with a 56-bit key can now be easily broken by an "exhaustive key search"
- DES is quite resistant to known analytical attacks, such as Differential Cryptanalysis and Linear Cryptanalysis.
- To strengthen the security of DES, DES encryption can be applied to the data three times in succession, which is called Triple DES (3DES).
3DES provides higher security, and there is currently no known practical attack that can effectively break 3DES. 3DES is still used in certain systems that require high security, but it is slower than AES.
- The default symmetric encryption algorithm: AES
 Nowadays, the "default" symmetric encryption algorithm is usually AES, because it is more secure and more efficient.
Besides AES, several other candidate algorithms that made it to the final round of the AES selection (such as Serpent, Twofish, etc.) are also considered very secure and efficient.
