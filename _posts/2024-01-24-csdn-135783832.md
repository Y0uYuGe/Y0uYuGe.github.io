# A Typical Data Communication Model
WANs contain analog channels; an analog channel can carry analog signals but not digital signals

Data leaves the computer's network card as a digital signal, is converted by a modem into an analog signal to be transmitted over the WAN's analog channel, then converted back into a digital signal by the modem, and only after the computer processes the digital signal is the data presented

The modem on the sending side: the transmitter
The modem on the receiving side: the receiver
The WAN in the middle is called the transmission system

# Terminology of Data Communication
Symbol sequence: can be thought of as a 0/1 sequence
Data can be regarded as the 0/1 bit-sequence representation of the message to be transmitted
A signal is the form the data takes during transmission
A channel has a direction

# Serial / Parallel
"Slower speed" means it takes longer for all the data to travel from the sender to the receiver
"Higher cost" means more channels and therefore more expense

Parallel transmission is used inside a computer
![Image](/images/csdn/2024/csdn-135783832/image-005-679d267464.png)
# Synchronous / Asynchronous
Synchronous: the last four blocks are the data to be transmitted; a synchronization character is sent first, like notifying the other side to prepare to receive, and then the two sides' clocks are synchronized (the sending intervals are identical)
Asynchronous: the sender adds a start bit and a stop bit. Suppose the start bit is 0 and the stop bit is 1; when the start bit is 0 the receiver's level jumps to 0, telling the receiver it can start receiving data, and when the stop bit is 1 the receiver's level jumps to 1, telling it to stop receiving data. This lasts until another start bit arrives and changes the level (the sending intervals differ, as with a keyboard)

# Two Ways to Express the Data Transmission Rate
       
   A binary symbol = one symbol per binary bit, or in other words the number of symbols equals the number of possible values of a binary number, i.e. 2
 
 Symbol length is the duration or width occupied by a symbol
 The symbol rate depends only on the symbol length
Two symbols of two-symbol width are two different symbols even if their waveforms look identical; think of what was sent: although the corresponding waveforms are the same, they are not the same thing
