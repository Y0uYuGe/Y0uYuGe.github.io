### Substitution Attack on CBC
- If a proper IV (initialization vector) is chosen for every transfer, the attack becomes completely ineffective. (The IV is a crucial parameter in CBC encryption mode. If a suitable IV is randomly generated for every transfer, the attacker cannot exploit fixed patterns to recognize or tamper with the transfer information.)
- If we choose a new IV every time we encrypt, CBC mode becomes a probabilistic encryption scheme, meaning that two encryptions of the same plaintext look completely different. (By using different random IVs, the ciphertexts produced by encrypting the same plaintext are completely different, which enhances security and prevents attackers from finding patterns by observing ciphertexts.)
- In general, the IV should be a non-secret random number (used only once). (The main role of the IV is to randomize the ciphertext; it can be made public, but it should be a random value and must not be reused.)

If the same IV is used for every transfer, an attacker can recognize that this transfer action has been sent many times; if it is their own transfer, they will know that this ciphertext corresponds to the encryption of the transfer plaintext
