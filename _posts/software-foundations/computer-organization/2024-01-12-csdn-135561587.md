# The development of hardware
## First generation
Logic element: the smallest basic unit for processing electrical signals
Vacuum tube: large, high power consumption
![Image](/images/csdn/2024/csdn-135561587/image-004-ef458c63d7.png)
Programmers at this time mostly programmed in machine language. As for "bug": a small insect once occupied a hole, causing the binary signals of machine language to be read incorrectly, which produced errors — hence errors are called bugs
![Image](/images/csdn/2024/csdn-135561587/image-005-06cce8766d.png)
## Second generation
Transistor: smaller than vacuum tubes, lower power consumption
![Image](/images/csdn/2024/csdn-135561587/image-006-7461bbb02c.png)
## Third generation
Electronic components are integrated at high density on a circuit board
![Image](/images/csdn/2024/csdn-135561587/image-007-53d8729b40.png)
## Fourth generation
With advances in integration technology, larger-scale integrated circuits appeared
Microprocessor: CPU

# The development of software
High-level languages no longer require much attention to the specific characteristics of the machine; you only need to focus on the problem to be solved.

DOS: Disk Operating System, a class of operating systems on early personal computers.
During the 15 years from MS-DOS 1.0 in 1981 to MS-DOS 6.22 in 1995, DOS, as an operating system vehicle used by Microsoft on personal computers, released multiple versions. DOS occupies a pivotal position in the IBM PC compatible market. It can directly manipulate and manage files on the hard disk, running in the form of DOS.
