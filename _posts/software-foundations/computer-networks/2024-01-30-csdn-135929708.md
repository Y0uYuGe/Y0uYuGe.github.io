# Transmission Media and Their Classification
The physical layer specifies electrical characteristics: it defines the data corresponding to electrical signals

# Guided Transmission Media - Twisted Pair
The main role of twisted pair is to transmit data and voice information. By twisting two conductors together in a specific way, it can effectively reduce electromagnetic radiation and crosstalk between adjacent pairs, and suppress external interference. This design makes twisted pair very effective for short-distance communication, especially in local area network (LAN) environments.

A twisted-pair cable usually consists of one or more such twisted pairs placed inside an insulating sleeve. Although twisted pair is limited in transmission distance, channel width, and data rate, its cost is relatively low, so it remains the preferred transmission medium in many applications.

In addition, twisted pair comes in many types, including shielded twisted pair (STP) and unshielded twisted pair (UTP), which have different pros and cons in different application scenarios. For example, shielded twisted pair offers better noise immunity, while unshielded twisted pair is more popular because of its lower cost.

The two insulating layers in the figure correspond to the two wires of a twisted pair

The main role of the shielding layer is to reduce the influence of external electromagnetic fields on power or communication lines and to prevent the line from radiating electromagnetic energy outward.
- Metal shielding: for medium- and low-voltage power cables without a metal sheath, a metal shielding layer is added in addition to the semiconductive shielding layer. The metal shielding layer is usually made of copper tape or copper wire wrapping and mainly serves to shield the electric field.

# Guided Transmission Media - Coaxial Cable
Sharing a single axis
A baseband signal is an unmodulated signal, usually low-frequency, while a broadband signal is a modulated signal that can span a wider frequency range

Good noise immunity, so even over long transmission distances the accumulated interference is not too great
It is used for high-rate data transmission because its outer shielding layer also reduces the outward radiation loss of the internal signal.

# Guided Transmission Media - Optical Fiber
If the light is not refracted out (when entering the cladding) but is directly reflected, the light loss is low

Light has a high frequency, so the signal it carries is naturally high too, and the corresponding bandwidth is high as well
![Image](/images/csdn/2024/csdn-135929708/image-004-5bababe67f.png)
Single-mode fiber needs no refraction, so its loss is small. The fiber diameter is small
In multimode fiber (with multiple incident light rays), if the distance is too long there is too much refraction and the excessive loss causes distortion, so it suits short-distance transmission
![Image](/images/csdn/2024/csdn-135929708/image-005-80eb932837.png)
Optical fibers are thin; to protect them, a cable generally bundles multiple fibers together with some protective layers
![Image](/images/csdn/2024/csdn-135929708/image-006-2cf8c65d23.png)
The relay distance is the maximum distance a signal can travel in an optical fiber during fiber transmission; this distance is limited by fiber-line loss and dispersion.

In addition, in wireless communications, relay technology adds relay nodes to forward wireless signals, thereby extending coverage and increasing link capacity. Here the relay distance refers to the distances from the base station to the relay station and from the relay station to the mobile station. This technique can replace one poor-quality long-distance link with two good-quality short-distance links.

Optical fiber has excellent resistance to lightning and electromagnetic interference. Details:

- Resistance to electromagnetic interference: the basic component of optical fiber is quartz, which does not conduct electricity and is therefore unaffected by electromagnetic fields. What travels in an optical fiber is an optical signal, not electric current, so fiber transmission strongly resists electromagnetic and industrial interference. This allows fiber to maintain stable communication even in strong electromagnetic-interference environments such as high-voltage lines and substations.
- Resistance to lightning: optical fiber is an insulator and is not afraid of lightning or high voltage; even in extreme environments, such as the electromagnetic pulse produced by an atomic bomb explosion, fiber communication lines can remain unobstructed.

Crosstalk interference: can be understood as two signal lines interfering with each other
Fiber communication has no crosstalk interference because its signal can hardly affect other fibers (or rather, it hardly leaks out, which naturally gives it good confidentiality)

For the same amount of data, fiber is smaller and lighter, because fewer fibers are needed

# Unguided Transmission Media
These three kinds of waves are divided according to their different frequencies, or wavelengths
Radio waves do not need a specific direction to communicate

Ground-wave microwave relay communication is a technology that uses microwaves for long-distance communication within line-of-sight (straight-line) range on the ground.

A ground-wave microwave relay communication system, also called a microwave relay system or microwave link system, uses the microwave band with wavelengths from 1 meter to 0.01 meters, converting and passing signals through line-of-sight ground relay stations. Because of the propagation characteristics of microwaves, this communication method relies mainly on straight-line propagation, so a series of intermediate stations — relay stations — must be established between the two terminal stations to pass the signal station by station. These relay stations process the received microwave signal (such as frequency conversion and amplification) and forward it to the next relay station, thereby achieving long-distance beyond-line-of-sight communication.

Satellite communication:
Long transmission distance, heavily affected by weather, so the probability of bit errors is naturally high
Wide bandwidth and large capacity: satellite communication uses wide frequency bands and can provide greater communication capacity, meeting the demand for high data rates

Broadcast communication is one transmitter sending information to multiple receivers, while multiple-access communication involves information transfer between multiple transmitters and one or more receivers

Satellite communication easily enables broadcast and multiple-access communication mainly because of its unique technical characteristics and advantages. Details:

- Wide coverage: a notable feature of satellite communication systems is their broad coverage. Because satellites are in high Earth orbit, their signals can cover most of the Earth's surface. This means that within the area covered by a satellite's signal, any two points can communicate, unrestricted by terrain or geography.
- Strong broadcast capability: satellite communication systems have very strong broadcast capability. One geosynchronous satellite can cover one-third of the Earth's surface, so three appropriately distributed geosynchronous satellites can achieve global communication except for the poles. This broadcast characteristic makes satellite communication ideal for distributing news, weather forecasts, educational and entertainment programs, and so on.
- Multiple-access methods: satellite communication supports multiple access, meaning multiple Earth stations can communicate with different destinations through the same satellite. This multiple-access technology handles signals from different Earth stations and, via the satellite transponder's relay, establishes communication links among multiple users.
- High flexibility: circuit setup in satellite communication is very flexible — overly concentrated traffic can be dispersed at any time as needed, and the same channel can be used for communication in different directions or different regions. This flexibility allows satellite communication to respond quickly to various communication needs and, especially in emergencies, to establish communication links rapidly.
